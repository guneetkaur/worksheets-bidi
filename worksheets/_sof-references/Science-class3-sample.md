# NSO Class III — SOF Pattern Reference
# Source: Compiled from SOF NSO sample papers and official pattern
# Last updated: 2026-04-25 (run "update SOF papers" to refresh from sofworld.org)

## Paper Structure (full SOF NSO paper)
- Section 1 (Science): 35 questions × 1 mark = 35 marks
- Section 2 (Achiever's Section): 5 questions × 3 marks = 15 marks
- Total: 40 questions, 50 marks, 60 minutes

## Our practice paper condensed format:
- Section A (Logical Reasoning): 4 MCQs × 1 mark
- Section B (Science Content): 12 MCQs × 1 mark
- Section C (Achiever's): 2 MCQs × 2 marks

## Section A — Logical Reasoning (same as IMO style)
Same pattern: series, analogies, odd one out, mirror images.
Science-flavoured analogies common: "Fish : Fins :: Bird : ___"  (a) Wings  (b) Legs  (c) Scales  (d) Feathers

## Section B — Science Content (topic-specific examples)

### Plants
- "Which part of the plant makes food using sunlight?"  (a) Root  (b) Stem  (c) Leaf  (d) Flower
- "A plant that has thorns and stores water in its stem is a ___"  (a) Creeper  (b) Cactus  (c) Shrub  (d) Climber
- "Which of these is NOT a use of plants?"  (a) Food  (b) Medicine  (c) Making glass  (d) Making paper
- "Roots help the plant to ___"  (a) Make food  (b) Absorb water  (c) Produce seeds  (d) Both a and b
- "A plant that winds around a support to grow up is called a ___"  (a) Shrub  (b) Herb  (c) Climber  (d) Tree

### Animals
- "Which animal lives both on land and in water?"  (a) Fish  (b) Frog  (c) Whale  (d) Crocodile — wait, correct = Frog (amphibian)
- "A group of fish is called a ___"  (a) Herd  (b) Flock  (c) School  (d) Pack
- "Which of these animals is a herbivore?"  (a) Lion  (b) Tiger  (c) Deer  (d) Fox

### Human Body
- "Which sense organ helps us to smell?"  (a) Eyes  (b) Ears  (c) Nose  (d) Tongue
- "Food gives our body ___"  (a) Energy  (b) Shape  (c) Colour  (d) Weight
- "We should brush our teeth ___ times a day"  (a) Once  (b) Twice  (c) Three times  (d) Once a week

### Environment
- "Which gas do plants absorb from air to make food?"  (a) Oxygen  (b) Nitrogen  (c) Carbon dioxide  (d) Hydrogen
- "Rain is a form of ___"  (a) Solid water  (b) Water vapour  (c) Liquid water falling from clouds  (d) Ice
- "Which season comes after summer in India?"  (a) Winter  (b) Spring  (c) Monsoon  (d) Autumn

### Simple Science
- "A shadow is formed when an object blocks ___"  (a) Air  (b) Water  (c) Light  (d) Sound
- "Pushing and pulling are types of ___"  (a) Energy  (b) Force  (c) Motion  (d) Speed

## Achiever's Section (harder, applied)
- Multi-fact questions: "Which TWO of the following are true about cactus plants? (i) They store water in leaves (ii) They have deep roots (iii) They grow in deserts (iv) They need lots of water"
- Cause-effect reasoning: "If all the leaves of a plant are removed, what will happen?"  (a) Plant grows faster  (b) Plant cannot make food and will die  (c) Plant will grow more flowers  (d) Nothing will change
- Cross-topic: "Mohan eats only rice and bread. What is he MOST LIKELY to lack in his diet?"  (a) Carbohydrates  (b) Proteins and vitamins  (c) Fats  (d) Water

## Style notes (from real NSO papers)
- Diagrams are common — the text says "Look at the figure" but in our text-only paper, describe the scenario instead
- Options often include one "close but wrong" answer that tests understanding vs. memorisation
- Achiever's often tests "what would happen if..." — cause and consequence
- Indian context: familiar animals (cow, crow, mango tree), seasons (monsoon), food (dal, roti)
- Common traps: "carbon dioxide vs oxygen" for plants, "herbivore vs carnivore", "climber vs creeper"
