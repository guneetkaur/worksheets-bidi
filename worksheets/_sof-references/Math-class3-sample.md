## Paper Structure
- Section 1 (Logical Reasoning): 10 questions × 1 mark = 10 marks
- Section 2 (Mathematical Reasoning): 10 questions × 1 mark = 10 marks  
- Section 3 (Everyday Mathematics): 10 questions × 1 mark = 10 marks
- Section 4 (Achiever's Section): 5 questions × 3 marks = 15 marks
- Total: 35 questions, 45 marks, 60 minutes (full SOF paper)

## Our practice paper uses condensed format:
- Section A (Logical Reasoning): 4 MCQs × 1 mark
- Section B (Math Content): 12 MCQs × 1 mark
- Section C (Achiever's): 2 MCQs × 2 marks
- Total: 20 marks, 25 minutes

## Section 1 — Logical Reasoning (IMO style)
Question types observed in Class III papers:
- Number series: "2, 4, 6, 8, ___" or "3, 6, 12, 24, ___"
- Odd one out: "Which does NOT belong: Triangle, Square, Circle, Apple?"
- Analogy: "Pen : Write :: Scissors : ___"  (a) Cut  (b) Draw  (c) Paint  (d) Sew
- Mirror image: "Which is the mirror image of the number 3?"
- Alphabet series: "A, C, E, G, ___"  (a) H  (b) I  (c) J  (d) K
- Pattern: What shape/number comes next in the given sequence?

Key: Options are always plausible — wrong options differ by 1 step or use a related concept.

## Section 2 — Mathematical Reasoning (topic-specific examples)

### Fractions
- "Which fraction is greater: 1/2 or 1/4?"  (a) 1/4  (b) 1/2  (c) Equal  (d) Can't say
- "If a pizza has 8 slices and Rahul eats 3, what fraction is left?"  (a) 3/8  (b) 5/8  (c) 1/8  (d) 8/3
- "Which of these shows one-third shaded?" [with figure reference]
- "1/2 of 16 = ___"  (a) 4  (b) 6  (c) 8  (d) 12

### Addition & Subtraction
- "345 + 278 = ___"  (a) 613  (b) 623  (c) 613  (d) 533
- "500 – 237 = ___"  (a) 263  (b) 273  (c) 237  (d) 253
- Word problem: "Priya had ₹450. She spent ₹125 on books and ₹75 on lunch. How much is left?"

### Multiplication & Division  
- "7 × 8 = ___"  (a) 54  (b) 56  (c) 58  (d) 63
- "48 ÷ 6 = ___"  (a) 6  (b) 7  (c) 8  (d) 9
- "A box has 6 rows of 7 eggs. Total eggs = ___"

### Measurement
- "Which is heavier: 1 kg or 750 g?"  (a) 1 kg  (b) 750 g  (c) Equal  (d) Can't say
- "1 metre = ___ centimetres"  (a) 10  (b) 100  (c) 1000  (d) 10000

### Time & Money
- "How many minutes in 2 hours?"  (a) 20  (b) 120  (c) 200  (d) 60
- "₹5.50 + ₹3.75 = ___"  (a) ₹8.25  (b) ₹9.25  (c) ₹8.75  (d) ₹9.75

## Section 3 — Achiever's (harder, multi-step)
- Multi-step word problem: "A shopkeeper had 240 apples. He sold 1/3 in the morning and 80 in the evening. How many are left?"
- Pattern + operation: "In the series 1, 4, 9, 16, ___, the next number is formed by ___"
- Applied geometry: "A square has perimeter 24 cm. What is the length of one side?"
- Tricky fractions: "Which is the SMALLEST? 1/2, 1/5, 1/3, 1/4"

## Style notes (from real papers)
- Wrong options are carefully chosen: often the result of a common mistake (e.g., adding instead of subtracting)
- One distractor is always "too big" and one "too small" — forces estimation
- Word problems always involve familiar Indian contexts: rupees, school, food, home
- Achiever's questions often have "which of the following is CORRECT" format with 2 true statements mixed in
